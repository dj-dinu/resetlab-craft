**What easy means here**

- Easy: a narrow first version built from forms, curated content, simple rules, saved records, and exports. It works for one person or an existing small group.
- Medium: parsing real files, audio playback, shared state, permissions, or compatibility creates meaningful engineering and support work.
- Hard: dependable audio inference, separation, deep DJ-library writes, or broad interoperability needs sustained evaluation and qualification.

These labels describe the stated first version, not the complete commercial product. Public accounts, uploads, subscriptions, payments, real-time collaboration, and moderation can move an Easy idea into Medium or Hard. Software can be easy while content curation or live event operations remain demanding.

**The six finalists**

**Practicecraft — best new fit for teaching and a small community.**

Help beginner DJs answer: what should I practice between lessons, and how do I know I am progressing? Start with ten short drills covering cueing, phrase counting, beatmatching, EQ swaps, transitions, and gig preparation. Each drill has a goal, a timer or checklist, a self-reflection prompt, and the teacher's explanation. Save a practice log locally and export it for a lesson. Use recording links for optional teacher feedback after the basic loop works.

Your first possible testers are your students or beginner DJs you already know; access to enough testers still needs confirming. The useful distinction is a DJ-specific learning path based on your actual lessons. General music-teacher software already handles assignments and practice. [My Music Staff](https://www.mymusicstaff.com/managing-student-assignments-progress-and-practice/)

First-version effort: Easy for curated drills and logs; Medium for private student accounts and feedback. Automatic analysis of mixing skill is a separate Hard project. Ongoing work is good teaching content and manual feedback. Possible support model: a free practice tool associated with lessons, workshops, or optional curriculum packs. Demand for paid packs remains untested.

Validation: give three to five learners the same short practice path. Observe whether they complete it without coaching and return for another session. Ask what they would otherwise use, and keep only the parts that improve their practice.

**Showflow — best fit for cultural and community events.**

Help an organizer turn scattered messages into one clear event plan. Start with segments, durations, assigned people, announcement notes, DJ track/version notes, load-in and soundcheck, and a phone-friendly view plus printable export. A delay adjustment can recalculate later segments locally. Add culturally specific Garba/Diwali templates only from experience and community review; different events have different conventions.

The reason to choose it should be the quality of the event cue sheet and templates. Luma already covers approvals, waitlists, check-in, and guest export; Partiful already offers invitations, guest questions, and party coordination. [Luma guest management](https://help.luma.com/p/managing-your-guest-list), [Partiful](https://partiful.com/)

First-version effort: Easy for one editor and an exported plan; Medium for shared live editing and dependable device synchronization. Ongoing work is template quality and event-day reliability. Possible support model: free personal plans, later paid team or template features if repeat use supports them. Link to the event's existing ticket/RSVP service.

Validation: use it for one real event, compare the preparation work with the previous process, and record whether the DJ, host, and organizer can each find their next action quickly.

**Gig Ready — fastest practical utility to test.**

Help a DJ or AV operator remember the complete setup and bring everything back. Start with reusable equipment bundles, required accessories, packed/loaded/returned checks, last soundcheck notes, and a printable emergency card. The advantage must come from tested setups and accessory dependencies: choosing a particular speaker or controller brings its matching cables, power supply, and backup items into the list.

This could start within Reset Lab Rentals and become a shareable tool for mobile DJs. Inventory platforms already exist; Booqable covers rental inventory and bookings. The narrow product is the preparation and return workflow for DJ/AV jobs. [Booqable features](https://booqable.com/features/)

First-version effort: Easy with saved templates and one operator; Medium with staff permissions, condition-photo uploads, and lending availability. Ongoing work is equipment templates and occasional device/export support. Possible support model: a free core, optional specialist packs, or an internal operational tool that improves the rental business. A subscription is not necessary for it to be useful.

Validation: test across three actual gigs or rentals. Look for fewer forgotten accessories, faster preparation, and clearer return records. If the same benefit is achieved with a simple checklist, keep it as a small module.

**Spectracraft — strongest existing technical foundation.**

This is already a local alpha, so the next worthwhile step is qualification and a small user beta. Its product promise is local full-track spectral inspection with evidence and preserved originals. It must keep the canonical scan_quality.py engine, --full-spectrum mode, and the user's grading rules; encoded bitrate is informational. HQ requires at least 30% of the whole decoded track duration to contain measurable signal around 20 kHz. The grade is spectral coverage, not proof of source authenticity or overall listening quality.

The project documents identify unfinished clean-machine/native platform and release checks. Finishing a dependable package can be more valuable than adding another analysis feature. Broader library management already exists in Lexicon, so keep the positioning focused on understandable measurement evidence. [Lexicon](https://www.lexicondj.com/)

Remaining effort: Medium to Hard release engineering, depending on advertised platforms and formats. First possible users: DJs and producers willing to inspect a small real batch and explain the output. Current source is GPLv3 only; any future commercial packaging must respect the project's license and dependency terms. Possible revenue models are optional convenience or support services, not assumptions about restricting the existing source.

Validation: follow the project's existing release gates; observe users selecting files, interpreting measured/ungraded results, locating evidence, and exporting a report. Do not create another quality scanner or change the canonical rule for this planning exercise.

**Vibe Crate Cards — easiest public resource that expresses your taste.**

Help a DJ or organizer find music suitable for a particular atmosphere. Start with five to ten carefully curated cards: coffee house morning, soulful warm-up, car meet groove, approachable dance energy, or a specific cultural-event segment. Each card includes exact track versions, mood, vocal content/language where verified, a suggested energy arc, source/store links, and why the selections work. Publish the track metadata and source/store links.

First-version effort: Easy as a curated public collection. Community submissions, accounts, and reputation systems come later. Ongoing work is the listening and curation; code does not replace that. Possible support model: free resource associated with workshops, a newsletter, or optional curated educational packs. It can succeed as a trusted resource without becoming SaaS.

Validation: share a small set with DJs who play these environments. Ask which card they actually used and what they changed. Measure useful saves, referrals, and return visits. Event crate planning and discovery digests could be modules within this product.

**dbt Change Explainer — best separate technical portfolio candidate.**

Help an analytics engineer explain a model change during review. Start with two manifest.json files and a supported schema-version range. Identify changed models, dependencies, tests, and descriptions; generate a clear summary of downstream items to review. It explains artifact changes, not semantic SQL correctness or proof that a deployment is safe.

dbt already provides manifests and state comparison. The specific value needs to be a useful review narrative and shareable result. [dbt manifest documentation](https://docs.getdbt.com/reference/artifacts/manifest-json), [dbt state selection](https://docs.getdbt.com/reference/node-selection/methods#state)

First-version effort: Medium. A local artifact-based version can avoid warehouse credentials. Support work is artifact-version compatibility and credible change explanations. Possible support model: open-source utility and optional team workflow features, subject to actual adoption. First possible users are dbt community members or colleagues you can reach; this is an inferred distribution route.

Validation: compare three real or permitted example changes with reviewers' expectations, inspect discrepancies, and see whether a reviewer uses the generated report in another review.

**Products I would defer or narrow**

Generic library management, key analysis, and auto-set planning already have mature implementations. Lexicon covers playlists/cues/grids, library cleanup, and sharing; Mixed In Key covers key/energy/cues; DJ.Studio provides transition planning. A future ResetLab tool needs a specific, evaluated advantage. [Lexicon](https://www.lexicondj.com/), [Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/learn-more/), [DJ.Studio transitions](https://dj.studio/transitions)

Generic RSVP, ticketing, teacher administration, rental management, and public community platforms are also crowded. Existing products can be linked or used around a more specific companion workflow. [Luma](https://help.luma.com/p/managing-your-guest-list), [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/193833/), [My Music Staff](https://www.mymusicstaff.com/managing-student-assignments-progress-and-practice/), [Booqable](https://booqable.com/features/), [Circle](https://circle.so/)

Snowflake already has cost exploration and budgets; dbt already publishes artifacts and state comparison; OPA already has testing and a Playground. Favor focused explainers, offline review, and community learning material. [Snowflake costs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/cost-exploring-overall), [Snowflake budgets](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/budgets), [dbt artifacts](https://docs.getdbt.com/reference/artifacts/manifest-json), [OPA policy testing](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/policy-testing)

A DJ booking marketplace, peer-to-peer rental marketplace, or general music social network can be straightforward to demonstrate but difficult to make useful. Recruiting both sides, stale availability, disputes, moderation, and recurring support are ongoing work. Start within an existing group and solve one useful task before broadening.

**A practical pilot**

Choose one primary tool. Describe the recurring task in one sentence, recruit a few reachable users, and inspect how they currently handle it. Build one complete loop: input, useful result, save/export, reuse. Test it in an actual lesson, gig, event, or review. Look for repeat use and a concrete reduction in confusion or work before adding accounts, payments, or AI features.

For the user's music suite, follow the saved staged roadmap and release gates. Community teaching/event experiments can be considered separately; this research does not alter that roadmap or authorize publication, metadata writes, or song organization.

No songs, tags, audio samples, artwork, Rekordbox records, account settings, or existing source files were changed for this research. No messages were sent to other people, and nothing was published.
